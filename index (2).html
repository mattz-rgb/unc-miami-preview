The matchup

A pregame breakdown from North Carolina's side.

I think North Carolina can beat No. 1 Miami, but only if it wins the possession battle, converts on third and fourth down and doesn't turn the ball over.

What gives me real hope is the schedule. This is Miami's second straight road game, and next Saturday it's at Notre Dame, which was No. 2 in the coaches poll as of Oct. 11 and has a grudge. Miami beat the Irish 27-24 last year and took the last College Football Playoff spot over them. State of the U called this week a classic trap game, and Mario Cristobal is clearly trying to head that off: "there has never been a trophy or parade for anybody halfway through the season."

The other side of that is Miami just beat Pitt 52-23, and a team that scores like this one doesn't have to be at its best to win by two touchdowns. Miami also let Florida State tie it three times before winning 43-35 in two overtimes, and Cristobal said he could "rattle off a million mistakes and errors." That's the crack I'm betting on.

His logs read like typos: 27 of 30 for 401 and five touchdowns at Stanford, 31 of 37 for 387 and four touchdowns against Pitt. He can run too, with a 34-yard touchdown scramble at Clemson.

Billy Edwards Jr. has not thrown an interception in either of the last two games, and the Syracuse day (27 of 44, 271 yards, two touchdowns, eight different receivers) was his most complete. My worry is the pocket. He was sacked 11 times in the first five games and was reportedly playing through a shoulder issue at Duke. He isn't on this week's injury report. He can't win a shootout with Mensah. He wins by getting the ball out on time to tight ends and checkdowns.

Malachi Toney is the best player on the field. The sophomore caught 109 passes for 1,211 yards last year, returns punts and has Heisman buzz. Against Pitt he set a program record with 16 catches, and 159 of his 194 yards came by halftime. Cooper Barkate is the deep threat. Joshua Moore is out for the season.

UNC's tight ends are the best part of its offense. Jelani Thurman (6-6) had a career-best seven catches against Syracuse and a fourth-down catch that kept the winning drive alive at Duke. Shamar Easter had a 75-yard seam touchdown at Pitt. Jordan Shipp started slowly (26 catches, no touchdowns through five games) but played 68 snaps against Syracuse. Miami's blitz-heavy early downs are the defense tight end screens are built to punish.

Where UNC should target

The five places I'd go after Miami.

Miami's interior pass protection

With Max Buchanan out at right guard, this is the softest spot in Miami's offense. Send Abou Jaoude and Lewis inside with stunts and make Mensah leave his first read.

Tight end seams and screens

Hetherman blitzes on first and second down. A 6-6 Thurman or a seam runner like Easter finds space there, and a couple of early screens slow the blitz.

Shots down the field

Lopez, Reynolds and Daniels all topped 295 yards against Miami. Edwards needs two or three clean shots to Shipp, Kekahuna or Easter.

Penalties and possession

Miami gives up 62.9 penalty yards a game, UNC 31.7. Miami went 10 of its first 12 on third down against Pitt, so UNC should hold the ball for six minutes at a time.

Fourth down and the kicking game

Syracuse converted four of seven fourth downs and UNC one of two. Verhoff is the safe option when going for it isn't right.

Where UNC should be concerned

The risks are easier to list than the opportunities, and a lot of them feed each other.

Mensah and Toney against a thin secondary

Miami throws for 340.7 yards a game, fourth in the FBS. UNC is missing Miller, Waters is questionable, and Cost and Waters both sat out last week.

Edwards staying upright

Eleven sacks in five games, five interceptions and a reported shoulder issue. Wilson and Lightfoot can turn a sack into a short field.

The run defense late in games

186 yards at Clemson, 241 to Notre Dame, 199 to Syracuse. Fletcher and Mallory wear defenses down.

Leads that disappear

UNC has held three double-digit leads this season (Clemson, Duke, Syracuse), lost two and needed a late drive to survive the third.

Depth and availability

Jordan Hall, Andrew Threatt and Peyton Waters are questionable and Tre Miller is out. Abou Jaoude, Edwards, Cost and Benjamin Hall aren't on the initial report.

The scoreboard math

Miami scores 49.0 a game and UNC 24.4. UNC has scored more than 30 once this year, in the 35-3 win over ETSU.

Off-field turbulence

Lombardi resigned, an internal investigation followed and the defensive coordinator situation has been murky since August.

How I think it breaks down

A scenario, not a forecast. This is what has to go right for UNC.

  1. Q1

    Miami 7, UNC 3

    Miami scores first. UNC answers with a patient drive and a Verhoff field goal and stays out of third-and-long.

  2. Q2

    UNC 10, Miami 10 at the half

    The pass rush arrives through Miami's interior. A third-down sack, a seam to Easter or Thurman, and UNC leads 10-7 before Miami's field goal.

  3. Q3

    17-17

    Miami leans on Mallory and Fletcher and scores first out of the half. UNC answers right away with a long, clock-eating drive and a Thurman touchdown.

  4. Q4

    UNC 34, Miami 31

    Miami goes up 24-17 and UNC ties it. Miami goes back in front 31-24, Edwards answers with a touchdown drive to tie it again, and Verhoff kicks the winning field goal. UNC gambles with pressure on the last Miami drive.

In this script Mensah throws for 300-plus yards, Toney tops 100, and UNC wins time of possession and draws far fewer penalties. It's a shootout, so it needs the pass rush to show up early.

The pick

UNC34
Miami31

A three-point UNC win that comes down to the final possession. Four reasons:

  • Miami already showed it can be beaten. It needed two overtimes against Florida State despite outgaining the Seminoles 583-443.
  • The schedule works against the favorite: a second straight road game with Notre Dame next.
  • UNC's strengths line up with Miami's weaknesses: 31.7 penalty yards a game against 62.9, a pass rush against a patched interior line, and tight ends against a blitz-heavy defense.
  • Kenan at night with a dry forecast is about as good a setting as UNC gets.

I'll be honest about the other side. Miami is the No. 1 team in the country and the favorite for a reason. UNC has lost two of the three games where it led by double digits, its secondary is injured and its run defense has been gashed. If Mensah has time or Toney gets loose, this turns into the double-digit Miami win most people expect. So this is a pick on the best version of the game for UNC, not the most likely one.
